# Starter maintenance

Keeping an established starter alive and ready to bake: how often to feed it, in what proportions, and where to keep it. The choice is mostly about storage temperature, which sets the feeding interval.

## Feeding intervals by storage

King Arthur's guide gives two regimes ([[king-arthur-sourdough-maintain]], "Maintaining your starter"): "For best results, feed your room-temperature starter twice a day, and your refrigerated starter at least once a week." The less often a starter is fed, the longer it takes to get ready for baking; a starter forgotten in the fridge "for months on end" can probably be revived, but "it will take nearly as long as creating a new starter from scratch." Reviving a neglected starter is covered under [[sluggish-starter]].

Maurizio Leo treats the fridge as a short-to-medium break, not a weekly routine ([[perfect-loaf-store-sourdough-starter]], "How To Store Your Sourdough Starter for Up to Three Weeks"): he does not like to leave his starter "for more than three weeks in the fridge because it usually takes longer to revive." For longer breaks he dries it ([[long-term-starter-storage]]).

King Arthur's storage post ([[king-arthur-how-to-store-sourdough-starter]], "Where to store sourdough starter", 2024-05-16) frames the choice by how often you bake: room temperature suits frequent bakers, the fridge suits occasional ones and "only requires feeding it once a week." It says a room-temperature starter "needs to be fed daily", where the maintenance guide says twice a day; the guide is the more specific of the two.

## Skipping a feeding without the fridge

For a missed feeding or a night away, Leo slows the starter rather than refrigerating it ([[perfect-loaf-store-sourdough-starter]], "How To Store Your Sourdough Starter For A Day Or Less"): his normal feed of 100 g flour, 100 g water and 20 g ripe starter becomes 100 g flour, 80 g water and 10 g ripe starter. Lower hydration and a smaller carryover both slow fermentation; cooler water or a cooler spot do the same. He prefers this to the fridge for small daily adjustments, asking "why place your starter in a 'stressful' situation?"

## The keep-and-feed routine

King Arthur's standard maintenance feed ([[king-arthur-sourdough-maintain]], "Refrigerator storage: Feed once a week"): keep 113 g of starter, discard the rest, and feed it 113 g each of water and flour, a 1:1:1 ratio by weight. For the fridge, the fed jar rests on the counter "until it starts bubbling (1 to 2 hours) before returning it to the refrigerator." At room temperature the guide repeats the same feed every 12 hours ([[king-arthur-sourdough-maintain]], "Room-temperature storage: Feed twice a day").

The guide measures starter by weight because its volume "can vary wildly" with thickness and stirring (section "Starter is difficult to measure by volume").

To build more starter for a recipe that needs over 227 g, feed without discarding, keeping the same proportions, until there is enough plus 113 g to keep (section "How to increase a starter").

Leo's fridge feed is stiffer: 20 g ripe starter, 100 g flour and 80 g room-temperature water, an hour on the counter, then the back of the fridge; the lower hydration "helps the starter hold up extremely well in the fridge" ([[perfect-loaf-store-sourdough-starter]], "Storing Your Sourdough Starter In The Refrigerator"). Out of the fridge, he warms the jar for an hour or two, feeds as normal, and does "at least 2, preferably 3, regular feedings" before building a levain (section "How to Revive Your Sourdough Starter From The Refrigerator").

King Arthur's storage post adds three details ([[king-arthur-how-to-store-sourdough-starter]], "Where to store sourdough starter"): leave the fed starter on the counter "for a few hours" before it goes back in the fridge (the guide says 1 to 2 hours); keep it away from warm spots such as "the very top shelf or the door"; and a day or two before baking, give it "a couple of feedings at room temperature (roughly every 12 hours)".

## Container

A lidded container large enough for triple the starter's volume, wide-mouthed for stirring, ideally clear to track the rise. The lid should fit securely but not be airtight, "because starters emit gasses that can build up in the vessel and cause a messy blowout" ([[king-arthur-how-to-store-sourdough-starter]], "What to store sourdough starter in"). The post sells crocks, so read its product mentions as such.

## Flour for feeding

An established starter does not need [[whole-grain-flour]]; all-purpose flour is fine for maintenance ([[king-arthur-sourdough-maintain]], "What about using whole grains?").
